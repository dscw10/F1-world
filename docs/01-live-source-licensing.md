# Live data sources, licensing, and whether a paid tier is possible

**Written:** 2026-09-07
**Settles:** nothing yet. This is the evidence for a decision in `context.md` §8.
**Status:** research complete, decision outstanding.

> **I am not a lawyer and this is not legal advice.** It is a survey of published
> licences and terms, plus the relevant precedent, assembled so that Chris knows
> which question to put to a solicitor rather than paying one to discover it.
> Money changing hands is what makes this real: a free hobby project and a paid
> product sit in completely different positions.

## 1. The headline finding

There is an inverse relationship between how comfortable a source is legally and
how good a product you can build on it.

The data that makes this product distinctive — car position at ~10 Hz, speed,
throttle, brake, from which micro-sectors, the explorer and every spatial
encoding are derived — is available in depth **only from the unofficial
sources**, and those are the ones that forbid commercial use. The sources you
can straightforwardly pay for and resell supply timing and classification: lap
times, pit stops, intervals, standings. That is the commodity layer, and it is
roughly what Apple TV already gives away.

So the licensing question is not a formality to clear on the way to building.
**It partly determines what the product can be.**

## 2. What Formula 1 claims

> "All results, timing data and certain other content are copyright Formula One
> World Championship Limited. Statistics, Results and Timing Data are protected
> by copyright and database rights." **[summary]**

Licensing enquiries go to **brandprotection@f1.com**, and F1 asks for a full
proposal including proposed fees and/or royalties. **[summary]**

### The claim is broader than the law may support

Worth knowing, because it changes what you are negotiating against.

In **British Horseracing Board v William Hill** the European Court held that the
database right does **not** subsist where the investment went into *creating* the
contents rather than *obtaining or verifying* them. Fixture lists and results
generated by the sport's own operation have repeatedly fallen on the wrong side
of that line for the rights holder. **[source]** F1's timing data is created by
F1's own timing operation, which is precisely the fact pattern the ruling
addressed.

**Do not read that as a green light.** Four things survive it:

1. **Copyright in the compilation or presentation** may subsist even where
   database right does not.
2. **Contract binds regardless of IP.** Accessing F1's own live timing stream
   means accepting F1's terms of use. That is a contractual question and the
   BHB reasoning does nothing about it.
3. **Later cases went the other way on facts.** The *Football Dataco* line found
   a website operator jointly liable for its customers' infringement.
   **[summary]**
4. **Trade marks are a separate and much clearer problem.** See §5.

The realistic summary: the underlying rights position is more contestable than
F1's blanket wording implies, but "contestable" means "you might win an argument
you cannot afford to have." For a solo paid product, the practical constraint is
the source's own terms, not the outcome of litigation nobody is going to fund.

## 3. The sources, assessed

| Source | Licence / terms | Live? | Telemetry? | Paid product allowed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **OpenF1** | **CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** **[source]** | Yes | Yes | **No.** NonCommercial *and* ShareAlike |
| **FastF1** | MIT — the *software* **[source]** | Recorder only | Yes | Software yes; the data it fetches, no |
| **jolpica-f1** | Apache 2.0 + Terms of Use **[summary]** | No | No | Probably — but it is not a live source |
| **F1 live timing stream** | F1's own terms | Yes | Yes | **No** without a negotiated licence |
| **Sportmonks** | Commercial, ~€79/mo **[summary]** | Yes | Timing, position, intervals — telemetry depth unclear | Yes. That is what you are buying |
| **Hyprace** | Commercial, from ~$7.99/mo via RapidAPI **[summary]** | Partial | No | Yes |
| **Official F1 licence** | Negotiated | Yes | Yes | Yes, at a price |

### OpenF1 — the decisive finding

OpenF1's repository licence is **Creative Commons Attribution-NonCommercial-ShareAlike
4.0 International**. I read this at source. Two clauses each independently block
a paid tier:

- *"reproduce and Share the Licensed Material, in whole or in part, for
  NonCommercial purposes only"* — a subscription product is commercial.
- *"The Adapter's License You apply must be a Creative Commons license with the
  same License Elements"* — ShareAlike is viral. Even setting the money aside,
  it would reach into what you build on top.

Their own site reportedly states the project is *"intended for educational
purposes, personal learning projects, research, and non-commercial fan
engagement"* and invites commercial enquiries: *"For other use cases, please
contact us to discuss appropriate licensing."* **[summary]**

Also reported: real-time access is itself a paid subscription with them (data
counts as live from 30 minutes before a session to 30 minutes after; outside
that window it is historical and free), with a free tier of 3 req/s and 30
req/min. **[summary]**

**The catch to raise with them:** OpenF1 disclaims owning F1 data — *"OpenF1 is
an unofficial project and is not affiliated with Formula 1 companies. All
F1-related trademarks are owned by Formula One Licensing B.V."* **[source]** So a
commercial licence from OpenF1 licenses *their* compilation and effort. It does
not, and cannot, resolve F1's underlying claim. Ask them directly what they
believe they are licensing you.

### FastF1 — a distinction that matters

FastF1 is MIT licensed. **[source]** MIT licenses **the code**, not the data the
code fetches. A permissive licence on a client is not permission to
commercialise what it downloads from someone else's server. This is the single
easiest mistake to make here, and it would be made in good faith.

FastF1 is nonetheless the right tool for the **archive** half of the project, and
for building and verifying every analytic. Its live-timing component is a
recorder that must run during a session, not a serving layer.

### The licensed commercial providers

Sportmonks (~€79/month, no contract) and Hyprace (from ~$7.99/month via
RapidAPI) sell F1 data commercially. **[summary]** These are the clean route to a
paid tier.

**The unresolved question is telemetry depth.** Sportmonks describes lap data,
pit stops, live position and interval data; whether that extends to per-sample
speed, throttle, brake and 10 Hz coordinates is not established. Hyprace appears
to be results, standings and session data — no telemetry.

**This is the first thing to verify, before anything else in this document.**
Both offer free tiers or instant tokens, so it costs an afternoon: subscribe,
pull a session, and see whether the channels the product needs are actually
there. If they are not, options B and D below collapse and the choice narrows
sharply.

## 4. The options

**A. Free product, unofficial data.** Build on OpenF1 and FastF1, charge
nothing. Fully viable today, zero licensing spend, keeps all the telemetry and
therefore the whole product. Costs you the paid tier and the AI tier with it.

**B. Licensed commercial data.** Sportmonks or similar. The paid tier is clean
and the cost is trivial against a subscription business. You probably lose
telemetry, and with it micro-sectors, the spatial layers and much of what makes
the explorer worth using.

**C. Negotiate a commercial licence with OpenF1.** They invite it. Cheapest route
that keeps telemetry *and* allows charging — but see the catch above: it may not
cure the underlying rights position, only OpenF1's own terms.

**D. Official F1 licence.** brandprotection@f1.com with a full commercial
proposal. Realistically priced for betting operators and broadcasters rather than
solo products, but one email establishes that rather than assuming it.

**E. Sequence it, and decide later.**

### Recommendation: E, then choose between A, B and C on evidence

**Nothing in WP1 to WP6 is blocked by any of this.** The archive pipeline, every
analytic, the design system, the dashboard, the explorer and the 3D circuit can
all be built on FastF1 archive data, for a race that finished two years ago, with
nothing being sold. That is the great majority of the work and it carries no
commercial exposure, because there is no commerce.

The licensing decision only binds at **WP7** (live transport) and **WP9** (the
paid tier). By then you will know whether the thing is worth paying to license,
which is a much better position to negotiate or choose from than now.

**Do these three things now, though, because replies take weeks and they cost
nothing:**

1. Subscribe to a Sportmonks or Hyprace free tier and establish what telemetry
   is actually available. This is the load-bearing unknown.
2. Email OpenF1's commercial contact. Ask what a commercial licence costs and,
   specifically, what they believe it licenses.
3. Email brandprotection@f1.com. Expect nothing; the answer is worth having.

## 5. The name is a separate problem, and a clearer one

F1 actively enforces its trade marks against creators, and has required channels
using "F1" commercially to rebrand. **[summary]** Trade mark is far more
straightforward than database right, and F1 has both the motivation and the
machinery.

**This repository is called `F1-world`.** A commercial product cannot ship under
a name containing "F1" or "Formula 1", and will need a disclaimer of affiliation
regardless. Worth settling early — a name is cheap to change now and expensive
once anyone has heard of it.

---

## 6. What to ask a solicitor

When it is time, and it is time before money changes hands, not after. Do not pay
for a general briefing; pay for these five:

1. Does a UK-based paid subscription product derived from F1 timing and telemetry
   infringe copyright or database right, given *BHB v William Hill*?
2. Does obtaining that data via a third party (OpenF1, or a commercial reseller)
   change the answer, and does an indemnity from them mean anything?
3. What disclaimer and naming avoids trade mark exposure?
4. If the data reaches us via a source bound by F1's terms of use, does their
   contractual breach reach us?
5. What is realistic exposure — a takedown, or damages?
